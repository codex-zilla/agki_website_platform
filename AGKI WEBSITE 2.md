# **Inadequate Infrastructure and Supply Chains** 

Many clinics and hospitals, particularly in rural areas, operate without beds, reliable electricity, running water, or basic surgical facilities. This is compounded by weak supply chains, resulting in chronic shortages of essential medications, vaccines, and diagnostic equipment. 



. 

. 



. Critical Shortage of Healthcare Workers 

Sub-Saharan Africa bears over 20% of the global disease burden but houses only a tiny fraction of the world's healthcare workforce. The region faces a severe shortage of doctors, nurses, and midwives due to limited training capacity and "brain drain" where trained professionals emigrate to wealthier nations for better pay and working conditions. 

. 



High Out-of-Pocket Costs & Lack of Funding 

Most countries in the region lack comprehensive health insurance systems. As a result, patients must pay directly for medical care, leading to extreme financial hardship or causing families to skip lifesaving treatment entirely due to cost.
